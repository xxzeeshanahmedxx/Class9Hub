## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Non-physical Quantity:** A quantity that cannot be measured.
- **Measurement:** The process of comparison of an unknown quantity with a standard quantity. It has two parts: magnitude and unit.
- **Least Count:** The smallest reading on an instrument, found by dividing the smallest main scale division by the number of vernier scale divisions.
- **Zero Error:** The error in an instrument when the zero of the vernier scale does not align with the zero of the main scale when the jaws are closed.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Three base quantities: Length, Mass, Time.
- Three derived quantities: Pressure, Force, Velocity (any three).
- Seven SI base units: meter, kilogram, kelvin, ampere, mole, candela.
- Prefixes used: Kilo (10³), Mega (10⁶), Giga (10⁹) for multiples; Centi (10⁻²), Milli (10⁻³), Micro (10⁻⁶) for sub-multiples.
- For constructed response: thickness of Rs.5 coin → millimeters, length of book → centimeters, distance between cities → meters or kilometers, mass of five coin → grams, mass of school bag → kilograms, duration of school period → hours or minutes, volume of petrol → liters.
- A standard system ensures accuracy, consistency, and clear communication.
- For measuring pencil diameter with meter scale: take multiple readings at different points and find average.
