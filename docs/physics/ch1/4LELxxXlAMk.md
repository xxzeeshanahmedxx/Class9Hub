# Numerical Problems 1.1 To 1.5 — Class 9 Physics Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This video covers the step-by-step solution of numerical problems 1.1 through 1.5 from Chapter 1 (Physical Quantities & Measurement). The teacher demonstrates how to apply scientific notation, SI prefixes, and basic arithmetic operations (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division) to solve physics numericals.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
The teacher solves each numerical in detail. **Problem 1.1** calculates the number of seconds in a day, week, and month using SI prefixes (Kilo, Mega). **Problem 1.2** converts those same answers into scientific notation. **Problem 1.3** covers addition and subtraction in scientific notation, emphasizing that powers of 10 must be equal before adding/subtracting — the teacher demonstrates converting exponents by moving the decimal point. **Problem 1.4** covers multiplication and division in scientific notation (powers don't need to match for these operations). **Problem 1.5** is a mixed problem involving unit conversions (kilometers to meters) combined with multiplication and division, ending with proper units (kg·m/s²).

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Scientific Notation:** Writing numbers as a × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ a < 10.
- **SI Prefixes:** Kilo (10³), Mega (10⁶) used to simplify large numbers.
- **Significant Figures:** Rules for decimal placement when converting between prefix and scientific notation.
- **Least Count:** When adding/subtracting in scientific notation, powers must be equal; when multiplying/dividing, powers are added/subtracted.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Decimal moving left → positive exponent; decimal moving right → negative exponent.
- For addition/subtraction in scientific notation, first make powers of 10 equal by moving the decimal.
- For multiplication: multiply coefficients, add powers of 10.
- For division: divide coefficients, subtract powers of 10.
- When converting to SI prefixes: aim for power of 3 (Kilo), 6 (Mega), etc.
- Always write the final answer with correct units.
- 1 Light Year = distance light travels in one year = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m (from problem 1.9 later context).

## What Students Should Remember
Numerical problems require written practice. Simply watching the solution is not enough — you must solve each problem yourself at least once or twice. Pay special attention to decimal movement rules and unit handling. The most common mistake is incorrect power of 10 when moving the decimal.
