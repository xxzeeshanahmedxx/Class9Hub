# Numerical Problems 1.5 To 1.10 — Class 9 Physics Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This video covers numerical problems 1.6 through 1.10 from Chapter 1 (Physical Quantities & Measurement). The teacher explains significant figures, scientific notation conversion, correct prefix usage, light year calculation, and unit conversion (density from g/cm³ to kg/m³).

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
**Problem 1.6** covers significant figures rules: non-zero digits are always significant, zeros between significant figures are significant, leading zeros (left side) are not significant, trailing zeros (right side after decimal) are significant, and the power of 10 in scientific notation is ignored when counting significant figures. **Problem 1.7** practices converting numbers into proper scientific notation (decimal must be after the first non-zero digit). **Problem 1.8** covers using correct SI prefixes — converting values so the power of 10 matches available prefixes (Kilo = 10³, Milli = 10⁻³, etc.). **Problem 1.9** calculates 1 Light Year: the teacher explains light year is a unit of distance (not time), gives speed of light c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, converts 1 year into seconds (365 × 24 × 60 × 60 = 31,536,000 s), then uses distance = speed × time to find 1 light year = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m. **Problem 1.10** converts density from 13.6 g/cm³ to kg/m³ by converting grams to kilograms (÷1000) and cm³ to m³ (×10⁻⁶), yielding 1.36 × 10⁴ kg/m³.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Significant Figures:** All digits in a measurement that are known with certainty plus one estimated digit.
- **Light Year:** The distance light travels in one year — a unit of distance, not time.
- **Speed of Light (c):** 3 × 10⁸ m/s, denoted by symbol 'c' in physics.
- **SI Prefix Conversion:** Moving decimal to match standard prefixes (Kilo, Mega, Milli, Micro, etc.).

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Significant figures rules: (1) All non-zero digits are significant. (2) Zeros between significant figures are significant. (3) Leading zeros are NOT significant. (4) Trailing zeros after decimal ARE significant. (5) Power of 10 in scientific notation is ignored.
- For prefix conversion: if the power doesn't match a standard prefix, adjust by moving the decimal one place at a time.
- Light year calculation: s = c × t (where c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, t = seconds in 1 year).
- Density conversion: 1 g = 10⁻³ kg, 1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³, so divide by 10³ and multiply by 10⁶ carefully.

## What Students Should Remember
Problem 1.9 (light year) is extremely important for exams — mark it in your textbook. The teacher emphasizes that numerical problems require written practice: watch, understand, then solve yourself. Practice each problem at least once or twice until you can solve it independently.
