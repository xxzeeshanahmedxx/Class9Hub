# Budding — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture covers Budding, an asexual reproduction method where a new organism develops as an outgrowth (bud) from the parent body. The teacher uses yeast and hydra as primary examples.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
Sir explains that in budding, a small projection called a **bud** forms on the parent organism. The bud grows, develops its own nucleus and organelles, and eventually detaches to become an independent organism. In **Yeast** (a unicellular fungus), budding is common: the nucleus divides mitotically, one nucleus moves into the bud, the bud enlarges, and separates. In **Hydra** (a simple multicellular animal sometimes discussed in plant reproduction context), a bud forms on the body wall, develops tentacles and a mouth, and detaches. The teacher emphasizes that the offspring is genetically identical to the parent.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Bud:** An outgrowth from the parent body that develops into a new organism.
- **Budding:** A form of asexual reproduction where a new individual arises from a bud.
- **Parent Cell/Organism:** The original organism producing the bud.
- **Detachment:** The separation of the mature bud from the parent.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Budding occurs in yeast (fungi) and some simple animals (hydra).
- The bud starts as a small bulge containing a nucleus.
- Unlike binary fission, the parent and bud are initially unequal in size.
- Buds may remain attached temporarily, forming colonies (in yeast).
- Offspring are genetically identical clones.

## What Students Should Remember
Listen carefully here: The difference between binary fission and budding is important. In binary fission, the parent splits into two equal halves. In budding, the parent remains and a smaller outgrowth forms.
