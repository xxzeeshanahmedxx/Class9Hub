# Fertilization — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture covers Fertilization in flowering plants, focusing on the unique process of Double Fertilization that occurs after pollination, leading to the formation of seeds and fruits.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
Sir explains that after pollination, the pollen grain germinates on the stigma. The **Pollen Tube** grows through the style toward the ovule, guided by chemical signals. The pollen tube carries two sperm cells. When it reaches the ovule, it releases the sperm cells into the **Embryo Sac**, which contains the Egg Cell and the Central Cell (with two polar nuclei). **Double Fertilization** occurs: One sperm fuses with the egg cell to form the diploid **Zygote** (which becomes the embryo). The other sperm fuses with the two polar nuclei to form a triploid (3n) **Endosperm** (which provides nutrition for the developing embryo). The teacher emphasizes that double fertilization is unique to angiosperms.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Pollen Tube:** A tube that grows from the pollen grain through the style to deliver sperm cells.
- **Double Fertilization:** The fusion of one sperm with the egg and another with the polar nuclei.
- **Zygote:** The diploid cell formed by sperm + egg fusion, developing into the embryo.
- **Endosperm:** The triploid (3n) nutritive tissue that feeds the developing embryo.
- **Embryo Sac:** The female gametophyte containing the egg and central cells.
- **Micropyle:** A small opening in the ovule through which the pollen tube enters.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Double fertilization is exclusive to flowering plants (angiosperms).
- One sperm + egg = diploid zygote (embryo).
- One sperm + two polar nuclei = triploid endosperm (food).
- After fertilization, the ovule becomes the seed.
- The ovary wall becomes the fruit (often ripens to attract seed dispersers).
- Seeds contain the embryo, endosperm, and seed coat.

## What Students Should Remember
Honestly, "double fertilization" is a unique concept you must remember. Most students remember only that sperm fuses with egg, but forget about the second fusion with polar nuclei. Both fusions are part of double fertilization.
