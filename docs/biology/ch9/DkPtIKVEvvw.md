# Transpiration — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture covers Transpiration, the process by which plants lose water vapor through their leaves. The teacher explains the types of transpiration, the mechanism involved, and why it is essential for plant survival.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
Sir defines transpiration as the evaporation of water from plant surfaces, mainly through stomata in leaves. There are three types: **Stomatal Transpiration** (90% of water loss, through stomata), **Cuticular Transpiration** (through the cuticle, minimal), and **Lenticular Transpiration** (through lenticels in bark, very small). The teacher explains the Transpiration Pull theory: as water evaporates from leaves, it creates a negative pressure that pulls water upward through the xylem from roots. This is called the Cohesion-Tension Theory—water molecules stick together (cohesion) and to xylem walls (adhesion), forming a continuous column.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Transpiration:** The loss of water vapor from aerial parts of plants.
- **Stomata:** Microscopic pores on leaf surfaces that control gas exchange and water loss.
- **Transpiration Pull:** The suction force that pulls water up the xylem due to transpiration.
- **Cohesion-Tension Theory:** The mechanism explaining water transport using cohesive forces between water molecules.
- **Guttation:** The loss of liquid water from leaf edges (not transpiration, occurs at night).

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Stomatal transpiration accounts for ~90% of total water loss.
- Transpiration pull is the main force moving water up tall trees.
- Cohesion (water-water attraction) and adhesion (water-xylem attraction) are essential.
- Transpiration also helps cool the plant and enables mineral transport.
- Guttation is the exudation of liquid water from hydathodes at leaf tips.

## What Students Should Remember
Pro tip: The cohesion-tension theory is an important exam topic. Remember the three key properties: cohesion, adhesion, and tension. Without these, water could not reach the top of tall trees.
