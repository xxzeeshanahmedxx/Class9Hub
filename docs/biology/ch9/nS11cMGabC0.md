# Internal Structure of Root and Uptake of Water and Salts — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture examines the Internal Structure of the Root and explains the mechanism of water and salt uptake from the soil into the plant's vascular system.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
Sir describes the internal structure of a root cross-section. From outside to inside: **Epidermis** (outer layer with root hairs), **Cortex** (storage area), **Endodermis** (selective barrier), **Pericycle**, and the central **Stele** containing xylem and phloem. The teacher explains that root hairs increase surface area for absorption. Water enters through osmosis (water moves from high concentration in soil to lower concentration in root cells). Mineral salts enter through active transport (energy required to move against concentration gradient). Once inside, water moves through two pathways: **Apoplast** (through cell walls) and **Symplast** (through cytoplasm).

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Root Hairs:** Tiny extensions of root epidermal cells that increase water absorption surface area.
- **Apoplast Pathway:** Water movement through cell walls and intercellular spaces.
- **Symplast Pathway:** Water movement through the cytoplasm via plasmodesmata.
- **Casparian Strip:** A waxy barrier in the endodermis that forces water into the symplast pathway.
- **Stele:** The central cylinder of the root containing vascular tissues.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Root hairs provide enormous surface area for absorption.
- Water enters roots by osmosis (passive).
- Mineral ions enter by active transport (requires ATP).
- The Casparian strip blocks the apoplast pathway, controlling what enters the xylem.
- The endodermis acts as a selective filter.
- Once water reaches xylem, it moves upward to the rest of the plant.

## What Students Should Remember
Well, the apoplast vs. symplast pathway is a tricky concept. remember: apoplast = between cells (faster), symplast = through cells (slower but regulated). the casparian strip forces water into the symplast.
