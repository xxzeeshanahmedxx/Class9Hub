# Plant Physiology Complete Introduction — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
Chapter 9 introduces Plant Physiology, the study of how plants function. This introductory lecture provides a broad overview covering plant nutrition, transport, gaseous exchange, and excretion mechanisms.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
The instructor explains that plant physiology covers everything plants do to survive—how they absorb water and minerals, perform photosynthesis, transport food, exchange gases, and excrete waste. Unlike animals, plants do not have specialized organs like a heart or lungs, yet they perform all vital functions through specialized tissues. Sir introduces the key topics: nutrition (how plants get food), transport (how water and food move), transpiration (water loss), gaseous exchange (breathing), and excretion (waste removal).

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Plant Physiology:** The branch of biology dealing with the functions and vital processes of plants.
- **Nutrition:** The process by which plants obtain and utilize food.
- **Transport:** The movement of water, minerals, and food within the plant body.
- **Transpiration:** The loss of water vapor from plant leaves through stomata.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Plants are autotrophs—they make their own food via photosynthesis.
- Water and minerals are absorbed by roots and transported upward.
- Food (sugar) is produced in leaves and transported to all parts.
- Stomata regulate gas exchange and water loss.
- Plants excrete waste through various mechanisms.
