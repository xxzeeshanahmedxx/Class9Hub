# Overview of Number of ATPs — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This concise lecture provides a numerical Overview of the Number of ATPs produced during cellular respiration, giving students a clear accounting of energy production across each stage.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
Sir provides a complete ATP count. **Glycolysis:** Produces 2 ATP (net) and 2 NADH. **Pyruvate Oxidation:** Produces 2 NADH (each yielding ~3 ATP). **Krebs Cycle:** Produces 2 ATP, 6 NADH, and 2 FADH₂. **Electron Transport Chain:** Each NADH yields ~3 ATP, each FADH₂ yields ~2 ATP. Total theoretical ATP per glucose = 36-38 ATP. However, the teacher notes that in reality, the actual yield is around 30-32 ATP due to energy costs of transporting molecules into the mitochondria.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Theoretical Yield:** The maximum possible ATP (36-38 per glucose).
- **Actual Yield:** The real-world ATP (30-32 per glucose) after accounting for transport costs.
- **NADH:** Electron carrier producing ~3 ATP in the ETC.
- **FADH₂:** Electron carrier producing ~2 ATP in the ETC.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Glycolysis: 2 ATP (plus 2 NADH → ~6 more ATP in ETC).
- Krebs Cycle: 2 ATP (plus 6 NADH → ~18 ATP, plus 2 FADH₂ → ~4 ATP in ETC).
- Total from one glucose: ~30-32 ATP in realistic conditions.
- Anaerobic respiration: only 2 ATP per glucose.
- The ETC produces the vast majority (~28-30 ATP).

## What Students Should Remember
Well, the ATP count is a common numerical question. remember the breakdown: glycolysis gives 2 ATP, krebs cycle gives 2 ATP, and the majority comes from the electron transport chain.
