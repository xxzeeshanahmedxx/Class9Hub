# Dark Reactions — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture covers the Dark Reactions (Calvin Cycle) of photosynthesis. The teacher explains how the ATP and NADPH produced in the light reactions are used to fix carbon dioxide into glucose.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
Sir explains that the Dark Reactions, also called the Calvin Cycle, occur in the stroma of chloroplasts. These reactions do not require light directly but depend on the ATP and NADPH produced during the light reactions. The process involves Carbon Fixation: CO₂ from the atmosphere is attached to a 5-carbon compound (RuBP) using the enzyme RuBisCO. Through a series of reactions, this is converted into 3-carbon molecules (PGA), which are then reduced to G3P (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate). Two G3P molecules combine to form one glucose molecule.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Calvin Cycle:** The series of biochemical reactions in the stroma that fix CO₂ into carbohydrates.
- **Carbon Fixation:** The incorporation of inorganic CO₂ into organic molecules.
- **RuBP (Ribulose Bisphosphate):** A 5-carbon compound that accepts CO₂ in the Calvin Cycle.
- **RuBisCO:** The enzyme that catalyzes carbon fixation, the most abundant protein on Earth.
- **Stroma:** The fluid-filled space inside the chloroplast where the Calvin Cycle occurs.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- The Dark Reactions do not need light directly but require ATP and NADPH from light reactions.
- Six turns of the Calvin Cycle produce one glucose molecule.
- RuBisCO is the key enzyme for carbon fixation.
- The Calvin Cycle regenerates RuBP so the cycle can continue.

## What Students Should Remember
Well, the difference between light and dark reactions is a very common comparison question. remember: light reactions = produce ATP/NADPH + O₂; dark reactions = use ATP/NADPH to make glucose.
