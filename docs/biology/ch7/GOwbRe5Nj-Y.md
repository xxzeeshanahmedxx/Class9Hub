# Introduction of Enzymes, Metabolism — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
Chapter 7 kicks off with the fascinating world of Enzymes. This introductory lecture defines enzymes as biological catalysts and explains the concept of Metabolism, the sum of all chemical reactions inside a living organism.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
The instructor defines enzymes as specialized proteins that speed up biochemical reactions without being consumed in the process. Sir explains Metabolism in two parts: Anabolism (building-up reactions, like photosynthesis and protein synthesis) and Catabolism (breaking-down reactions, like digestion and respiration). The teacher emphasizes that enzymes are absolutely essential for life because without them, metabolic reactions would occur far too slowly to sustain life.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Enzyme:** A biological catalyst, typically a protein, that accelerates chemical reactions in living organisms.
- **Metabolism:** The total sum of all chemical reactions occurring within a living organism.
- **Anabolism:** Metabolic pathways that build complex molecules from simpler ones, requiring energy.
- **Catabolism:** Metabolic pathways that break down complex molecules into simpler ones, releasing energy.
- **Catalyst:** A substance that speeds up a chemical reaction without being permanently changed.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Enzymes are substrate-specific—each enzyme works on only one specific substrate.
- Enzymes lower the activation energy required for a reaction.
- Most enzymes are globular proteins (except some ribozymes made of RNA).
- Metabolism = Anabolism + Catabolism.

## What Students Should Remember
Pro tip: The difference between anabolism and catabolism is a guaranteed MCQ. Remember: "Ana" = "build up" (think: Anabolic steroids build muscle); "Cata" = "break down" (think: Catastrophe breaks things apart).
