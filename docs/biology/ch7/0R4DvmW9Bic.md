# Mechanism of Enzyme Action — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture dives deep into the exact Mechanism of Enzyme Action, explaining step-by-step how an enzyme binds to its substrate and converts it into a product, including the induced fit model and the concept of activation energy.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
The instructor explains two models of enzyme action: the Lock and Key model and the newer Induced Fit model. In the Lock and Key model, the active site is perfectly shaped for the substrate. In the Induced Fit model, the enzyme slightly changes shape upon substrate binding to create a perfect fit. Sir then walks through the stepwise mechanism: Substrate approaches enzyme → Substrate binds at active site → Enzyme-substrate complex forms → Reaction occurs → Products are released → Enzyme is ready for the next reaction.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Enzyme-Substrate Complex:** The temporary intermediate formed when a substrate binds to an enzyme's active site.
- **Activation Energy:** The minimum energy required to start a chemical reaction.
- **Induced Fit Model:** A model where the enzyme's active site changes shape slightly to better fit the substrate after binding.
- **Transition State:** The unstable high-energy state during the conversion of substrate to product.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Enzymes lower the activation energy, making reactions happen faster and at body temperature.
- Without enzymes, reactions would need extremely high temperatures or strong acids.
- The active site contains specific amino acid residues that facilitate the reaction.
- After product release, the enzyme returns to its original shape unchanged.

## What Students Should Remember
Pro tip: The steps of enzyme action (E + S → ES → E + P) must be memorized perfectly. board exam short questions frequently ask you to label the enzyme, substrate, and active site in a diagram.
