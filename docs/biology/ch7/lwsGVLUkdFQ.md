# Characteristics of Enzymes — Class 9 Biology Lecture Summary

## Lecture Overview
This lecture builds on the introduction by detailing the specific Characteristics of Enzymes, explaining what makes these biological catalysts unique and how they function at a molecular level.

## Main Concepts Explained by the Teacher
The instructor explains that enzymes are proteins with a specific three-dimensional shape featuring an active site. Sir covers the key characteristics: enzymes remain unchanged after a reaction, they are highly specific (lock-and-key model), they work best at an optimal temperature and pH, and they are required in very small quantities because they can be reused. The teacher also explains that each enzyme has a specific substrate that fits into its active site like a key fits a lock.

## Important Definitions and Terms
- **Active Site:** The specific region on an enzyme where the substrate binds and the chemical reaction occurs.
- **Substrate:** The specific molecule upon which an enzyme acts.
- **Product:** The resulting molecule(s) formed after an enzyme-catalyzed reaction.
- **Lock and Key Model:** A model explaining enzyme specificity where the active site is perfectly shaped for one specific substrate.

## Key Points and Lists from the Lecture
- Enzymes are not used up in reactions—they can be reused thousands of times.
- Enzyme activity is affected by temperature, pH, and substrate concentration.
- Enzymes are specific—one enzyme typically catalyzes only one type of reaction.
- The suffix "-ase" is typically used to name enzymes (e.g., lipase, protease, amylase).

## What Students Should Remember
Honestly, the lock and key model of enzyme action is the most important concept here. if an exam question asks "explain enzyme specificity", you must describe how the active site only accommodates a specific substrate shape.
